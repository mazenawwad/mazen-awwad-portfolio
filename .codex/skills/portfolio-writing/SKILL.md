# Portfolio Writing

Write clear, credible portfolio copy that helps recruiters and engineering
leaders understand Mazen's work quickly.

## Source Discipline

- Treat `src/content/portfolio.ts` and the supplied CV as the source of truth.
- Preserve names, dates, roles, technologies, links, metrics, and scope exactly.
- Do not infer impact, ownership, seniority, scale, outcomes, or availability.
- Ask for missing facts when a stronger statement would require evidence.
- Keep portfolio strings in the typed content module unless the text is purely
  structural interface copy.

## Writing Standard

- Lead with the concrete work, responsibility, or outcome already supported.
- Prefer specific verbs over adjectives and generic claims.
- Use plain English and explain specialized context only when it aids scanning.
- Keep sentences direct and vary their length without becoming conversational.
- Remove filler, repetition, throat-clearing, and stacked buzzwords.
- Avoid inflated language such as "world-class," "cutting-edge," "expert," or
  "revolutionary" unless directly supported by the source.
- Preserve meaningful technical detail instead of replacing it with vague copy.
- Use parallel grammar across repeated items and timeline entries.
- Keep headings distinctive, literal, and connected to their section content.

## Interface Copy

- Make calls to action describe the destination or action.
- Keep navigation labels short and stable.
- Write metadata for search readers without keyword stuffing.
- Keep labels understandable without relying on nearby visual context.
- Make alternative text identify purpose or subject without invented visual
  description.
- Do not use explanatory UI copy to narrate animations or obvious controls.

## Editing Workflow

1. Identify the audience and the copy's job in the page narrative.
2. Trace every factual statement to the typed content or supplied CV.
3. Remove duplicated ideas before rewriting sentences.
4. Rewrite for clarity, specificity, rhythm, and scanability.
5. Check terminology, capitalization, tense, and parallel structure.
6. Read the copy in its rendered context at desktop and mobile widths.
7. Confirm that the revision introduces no new claim.

## Final Review

- Verify that the strongest information appears first.
- Verify that headings and supporting text do not repeat each other.
- Verify that paragraphs remain readable on narrow screens.
- Verify that all public details were explicitly approved by Mazen.
